01

Account-list scanner

Software and AIAccount research for sales and marketingPublic DNS recordsTry it yourself

Reads the public DNS records of any list of companies and shows which sales, marketing and AI tools each one runs, who is switching, and whose email is about to break. I ran it on 169 of the top startups from the Forbes, LinkedIn and CNBC lists.

169companies from three startup lists
80+public records read per company
128 vs 111companies that set up Claude vs ChatGPT
Dailyre-scan, and a change counts only when a second read agrees

What it does

Most go-to-market tools only work once a company points part of its DNS at them. A landing page on Marketo, an events site on Goldcast, a sending service in SPF, a domain verified with ChatGPT. The scanner reads those records and turns them into the three answers a sales team wants about an account. What it uses, whether it is changing, and whether it has a problem the team can fix.

The first scan says the newest startups set up Claude before they buy marketing software. 62% of LinkedIn's 2026 list has Claude, and 46% runs a marketing platform. In the Cloud 100 it is the other way round.

Open the scanner, or check a single company with the email DNS check.

How it works

  1. Ask public DNS about 80 questions per company, covering marketing subdomains, mail servers, SPF, DKIM, DMARC and verification records.
  2. Match each answer to a vendor and keep the record behind it, so every claim links to its source.
  3. Skip anything that only answers because a company points every name at one server.
  4. Re-scan every morning and log a change only when a second read agrees.
  5. Run the same code live in a visitor's browser on any list they paste.
02

Issue Scribe

Hardware manufacturingAI-assisted quality managementSample dataTry it yourself

A product demo of quality software for a hardware production line. A technician reports a defect with a photo and one line, an assistant drafts the report and shows the records behind every suggestion, and a named engineer signs off before anything changes.

4 rolestechnician, quality, design and program, each with its own work queue
1 clickfrom one bad part to every unit built with the same supplier lot
5 draftsreport, decision, corrective action, customer notice and answers, each citing its records
2 minutesfor the guided path, in your browser

What it does

Hardware teams log defects as nonconformance reports. Filing one is not the hard part. The hard part is noticing that three leaks in five weeks came from the same valve lot, finding every unit that lot went into, and stopping the ones still on the floor before another one ships.

In the demo, a leak at test is the third from one supplier lot. The first two were repaired and closed one at a time. The assistant connects them, compares that lot with a clean one, and proposes sending the part back and containing the lot. A quality engineer approves, nine units go on hold, and the one that already shipped gets a customer notice.

I first built it on Lovable in April 2025, in an afternoon. This version rebuilds it around what factory software is expected to show in 2026. An assistant that drafts the paperwork, evidence behind each suggestion, a person approving every change, and a full audit trail.

Open Issue Scribe and follow the 2-minute path on its home page.

How it works

  1. Keep one set of records (parts, supplier lots, 20 serialized units and what went into each), so every screen agrees.
  2. Draft each report from the unit's build record and the technician's note, and show why each field was filled in.
  3. Propose a disposition from past reports on the same part and lot, cite them, and change nothing until a person approves.
  4. Require a design engineer's sign-off when a part is used off drawing, unless an approved standard repair covers it.
  5. Log every action with who took it, and mark the ones the assistant proposed.

What the assistant does

Drafts the report

From a one-line note and the unit's build record, it fills in the part, supplier lot, test step and severity, and says why it picked each one.

Proposes the decision

It reads past reports on the same part and lot, compares lots, and checks which approved repairs apply. Then it recommends how to handle the part, from rework or an approved repair to sending it back to the supplier, and cites what it used.

Spots repeat problems

It flags when one defect keeps coming from the same lot, even after each report was closed on its own, and shows every unit that lot went into, including ones already shipped.

Writes the follow-up

It drafts the corrective action report and the hold notice to the customer from the same records, for a person to edit and send.

Answers questions

Ask which supplier cost the most this quarter, or where a lot was used, and get a table linked to the records with a line on how it was worked out.

Shows its work

Every run lists the steps it took before it writes. Nothing is saved until a named person approves it, and the activity log shows who approved what.

In the demo the assistant is simulated. It follows written rules over the sample data, shows its steps the way a live model would, and is labeled that way. A live version would send the same records to a model and keep the same evidence and approval steps.

03

Federal-contract pipeline

Defense and governmentOpportunity pipeline for business developmentSAM.gov daily file

Built overnight for a business operations interview at a defense hardware startup. It turns the federal government's full list of open opportunities into a short list a small team can act on.

72,060open federal postings scanned
203touch the company's technology
35rated 4 or 5 out of 5 for fit
Nightlypull at 4 AM, no API key needed

What it does

SAM.gov's search API allows ten calls a day, so the tool pulls the full daily file instead. It filters every posting against the company's own vocabulary, rates fit from 1 to 5, and suggests a move, from submit or shape it to partner, watch or pass.

Fit comes from a four-question scorecard. Does the product solve it, does the buyer have money, how fast could it be awarded, and is there a path to production. Past awards from USAspending nudge the buyer question up or down.

How it works

  1. Download the daily file and keep it, so every number traces back to the source posting.
  2. Exclude what the company can't sell into, like spare-parts buys and set-asides it doesn't qualify for.
  3. Score the rest by term matches, then band the scores into five plain fit levels.
  4. Classify the "door" (prototype deal, research call, early signal, solicitation) and the deadline.
  5. Let the team mark Pursue, Watch or Pass, keyed to the solicitation number so decisions survive each refresh.

Onboarding CRM

Software and AICustomer onboarding and follow-upTeam tracker and Slack

Built for an AI startup's community team to work through its post-launch waitlist. Every person who needs a touch lands in a queue named for the next action.

1,100+signups synced from the team's tracker
6action queues, from "invite" to "tell them the bug is fixed"
~1 centfor an LLM-drafted follow-up

What it does

It pulls signups from the team's existing tracker and issues from whole Slack threads, then sorts people into queues like nudge an unused invite or check in with someone going quiet. An LLM writer drafts each follow-up in the team's voice for a person to approve, and a Monday digest sums up the week.

What I'd build next

Product usage per account, so health scores come from real activity instead of a placeholder. Then send and track from the same screen, and close the loop when an issue a user reported gets fixed.

How I build

I decide what each tool should do, the rules it follows, and what counts as right. AI coding agents write the code. I check the output against real data before I rely on it, and I keep the raw records so any number on the screen traces back to where it came from.
